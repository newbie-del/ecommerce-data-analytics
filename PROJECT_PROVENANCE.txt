 4. THE FOUR STRUCTURAL DEFECTS  (the substantive analytical work)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  (a) EVERY CUSTOMER IS RECORDED TWICE.
      All 795 customer names carry EXACTLY two customer_ids. The split is
      geographic: one id serves APAC/EU/LATAM/US, the other Africa/EMEA.
      Aaron Bergman is both AB-10015 and AB-15.
      IMPACT IF MISSED: 1,590 "customers" instead of 795; every customer's
      lifetime value halved and split across two rows; RFM, CLV, cohort and
      retention analysis all wrong.
      FIX: resolve to the person via customer_key; retain customer_id so raw
      records stay traceable.

  (b) ORDER IDs ARE REUSED.
      659 order_ids appear against two different customers on two different
      dates (2,701 lines). order_id alone yields 25,035 "orders";
      (order_id, customer_id, order_date) yields 25,754 with zero remaining
      collisions.
      IMPACT IF MISSED: average order value overstated by 2.9%.

  (c) DISCOUNT CARRIES FLOAT ARTIFACTS *AND* REAL 3-DECIMAL TIERS.
      0.15000000000000002 and 0.44999999999999996 are binary-representation
      noise. But 0.002, 0.202, 0.402 and 0.602 are GENUINE discount levels on
      629 order lines.
      FIX: round to 3dp (29 raw values -> 27). Rounding to 2dp - the reflexive
      choice - would collapse those tiers into 0.00/0.20/0.40/0.60 and destroy
      a real pricing distinction on 629 rows.

  (d) THE RETURNS SHEET USES A DIFFERENT MARKET LABEL AND COVERS ONLY 4 OF 7
      MARKETS.
      Returns says "United States"; Orders says "US". And there are ZERO return
      records for Africa, Canada and EMEA (5,037 orders).
      FIX: normalise the label, then join on (order_id, market). Joining on
      order_id alone flags 3,050 lines; the correct composite join flags 3,043 -
      the 7 extras are false positives created by defect (b).
      AND: return rate is computed ONLY over the four covered markets. Counting
      the unmeasured markets as zero would report 4.67% instead of the true
      5.81% - a 1.14pp understatement - and invent a flawless returns record
      for three markets that were never measured.

  ALSO INVESTIGATED AND RESOLVED:
      38 (order_id, product_id) collisions were inspected INDIVIDUALLY rather
      than dropped. 28 are legitimate split lines with different quantities;
      9 differ ONLY in shipping cost (one order despatched as two parcels);
      1 belongs to a different customer on a different date. ZERO unexplained.
      A blanket drop_duplicates() would have deleted real revenue.

      457 product_ids carry more than one product name, and 1,944 product names
      are reused across different ids. product_id is used as the key because it
      never crosses a category boundary; product_label supplies one canonical
      name per id, and products.csv flags the ambiguous ids.

 5. WHAT IS REAL vs DERIVED  (read this before quoting any number)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  REAL, straight from the source:
    revenue (Sales), profit, quantity, discount, shipping_cost, order_date,
    ship_date, ship_mode, order_priority, category, sub_category, product name,
    customer name, segment, city, state, country, market, region, and RETURNS.

  DERIVED EXACTLY (no assumption):
    cost              = revenue - profit          (profit is real, so cost is
                                                   a measurement, not a model)
    list_revenue      = revenue / (1 - discount)
    discount_amount   = list_revenue - revenue

    The "revenue is NET of discount" relationship was TESTED, not assumed:
    across 6,671 products sold both at full price and at a discount, the NET
    model reproduces observed unit price with 0.0% median error, versus 40% if
    revenue were gross. See reports/data_quality_report.md section 10.

  DERIVED WITH A STATED LIMITATION:
    brand             = first token of product_name. Reliable in aggregate -
                        467 brands, 78 of them on 100+ lines covering 91.8% of
                        rows, only 0.22% of lines fall back to "Unknown". BUT
                        multi-word brands truncate ("Binney" for "Binney &
                        Smith") and 3.7% of product_ids disagree internally.
                        Labelled a derivation everywhere it appears.
    signup_date       = each customer's FIRST OBSERVED order. Anyone trading
                        before 2011-01-01 is left-censored, so tenure is a
                        floor, not a fact.
    "delivery" time   = order-to-SHIP lag. The workbook has no delivery date.
                        Reported under the honest name throughout.

 6. THE MOST IMPORTANT ANALYTICAL CATCH  -  IT IS A CLOSED PANEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The brief assumes a normal e-commerce business. This dataset is not one. It is
  a CLOSED PANEL of 795 customers observed for 48 months:

    - Minimum orders per customer : 15   (median 32, max 47)
    - One-time customers          : 0
    - Customers acquired after
      2011                        : 0    (all 795 first ordered during 2011)
    - Customers dormant >180 days : 1    (median dormancy 16 days)
    - Top 20% share of revenue    : 30.0%  (Gini 0.180)

  CONSEQUENCE - four metrics the brief asks for carry NO information here, and
  are reported as degenerate rather than shipped as flat charts:
    * "Repeat purchase rate"    -> 100% by construction
    * "One-time customers"      -> zero exist
    * "New vs returning"        -> 0% new in 2012, 2013 and 2014
    * Churn / "Lost customers"  -> no churn signal; 1 customer beyond 180 days

  CONSEQUENCE - the 80/20 rule DOES NOT HOLD. The top 20% of customers hold
  30% of revenue, not 80%. The standard recommendation "protect your top 20%"
  has nothing to attach to, and was explicitly NOT given.

  CONSEQUENCE - cohort retention does not decay (trend -0.21pp/month, p = 0.09,
  not significant across 47 months). Observed mean retention 43.8% matches the
  ~49% Poisson probability of at least one order per month implied by the
  average purchase rate of 0.67 orders/month. The cohort grid is measuring
  PURCHASE CADENCE, not survival. Presenting a flat heatmap as strong retention
  would be a misreading; presenting a decay curve would require inventing one.

  A BUG THIS CAUGHT IN MY OWN CODE: the first RFM implementation used low
  relative frequency as a proxy for "new customer" and assigned 91 customers to
  "New Customers" - every one of them acquired in 2011 with 17-31 orders. The
  rule was changed to gate on acquisition TENURE, which correctly returns ZERO.
  The empty segment is shown rather than deleted.

 10. OTHER CAVEATS (all stated in the README too)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - SHIPPING vs PROFIT, UNRESOLVED: whether shipping_cost is already deducted
    inside the recorded profit cannot be determined from this extract. Shipping
    is 10.70% of revenue, so netting it again would cut margin from 11.61% to
    0.91%. Profit is reported exactly as recorded; shipping is reported
    separately; profit_after_shipping exists only as an explicitly labelled
    scenario column and is never used as a headline. This ambiguity is real and
    is not resolved by this project.

  - Association, not causation. Discount correlates with collapsing margin and
    not with volume; that is not proof that cutting discount raises profit by
    the full amount. Recommendation 2 is specifically a controlled test.

  - CURRENCY IS ASSUMED to be a single consistent unit (USD). The workbook has
    no currency column despite covering 147 countries. If the source mixed
    currencies, cross-market comparisons would be affected. Nothing in the data
    lets this be checked.

  - Returns record only WHETHER an order came back, never why. No return-reason
    analysis is possible.

  - Only 4 years, and a closed panel. Nothing here supports a forecast.

  - RFM segment labels are RELATIVE positions within this panel. "Lost
    Customers" means a median of 49 days since last order, not churn. Every
    segment ships with its actual ranges and a label_caveat column.

  - SQL was executed on SQLite, not MySQL. MySQL 8.0 is installed and running
    on this machine but no credentials were available (ERROR 1045). The SQL is
    written in portable ANSI form with all date parts precomputed specifically
    so it runs unmodified on MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQLite and DuckDB. One
    portability bug was found and fixed during this work: a median threshold
    written as `rn = (n+1)/2` works on SQLite (integer division) but returns an
    EMPTY set on MySQL (5146.5 matches no row). It is now `rn*2 = n+1 OR
    rn*2 = n+2`.

  - SQL RFM vs Python RFM may differ by a customer or two at quintile
    boundaries, because NTILE and pandas qcut break ties differently. Aggregate
    totals agree exactly. Verified within a tolerance and disclosed rather than
    forced.
