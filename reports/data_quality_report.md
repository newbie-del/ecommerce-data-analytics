# Global Superstore - Data Quality Report

Generated by `src/data_cleaning.py`. Every number here is computed from
`data/raw/Global Superstore.xls` and is reproducible by re-running the script.

- Source sheets: `Orders` 51,290x24, `Returns` 1,173x3, `People` 13x2

## 1. Missing values

Across 1,230,960 cells, 41,296 are missing (3.35%), confined to:

| Column | Missing | % of rows |
|---|---:|---:|
| `postal_code` | 41,296 | 80.51% |

`postal_code` is absent for every non-US row. This is structural, not a
data error: the field only exists in the US market. It is **not imputed** -
inventing postcodes for 147 countries would be fabrication. The column is
kept as-is and simply unused outside US-level analysis.

## 2. Duplicates

- Exact duplicate rows: **0**
- Duplicates on all business fields (ignoring `order_line_id`): **0**
- `(order_id, product_id)` collisions: **38**

The collisions were inspected individually rather than dropped:

| Group type | Count |
|---|---:|
| Different quantity/revenue - a legitimate split line | 28 |
| Same money, different customer or date | 1 |
| Same money, shipping cost only (split shipment) | 9 |

**Decision: drop nothing.** 0 groups are unexplained, so there is no evidence of a genuine duplicate record. `order_line_id` (the raw `Row ID`) is unique across all rows and is the line-level primary key.

## 3. Customer identity - the most consequential defect

- Distinct `customer_id`: **1,590**
- Distinct `customer_name`: **795**
- IDs per name: {2: np.int64(795)} - i.e. *every* name has exactly two ids
- `customer_id`s spanning more than one segment: **0**

Worked example - **Aaron Bergman**:

| customer_id | markets | lines | revenue |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `AB-10015` | APAC, EU, LATAM, US | 77 | 20,037.50 |
| `AB-15` | Africa, EMEA | 12 | 4,607.13 |

The split is geographic: one id serves APAC/EU/LATAM/US, the other
Africa/EMEA. These are one person, recorded twice.

**Why this matters.** Grouping by `customer_id` would report 1,590 customers where 795 exist, split each person's true lifetime value across two rows, and make every genuinely loyal customer look like two lower-value ones. RFM segments, retention, cohort curves and CLV would all be wrong. **Decision:** resolve to the person via `customer_key`, and retain `customer_id` so the raw records remain traceable.

## 4. Order identity

| Candidate key | Distinct groups | Groups spanning >1 customer | >1 date |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `order_id` | 25,035 | 659 | 659 |
| `order_id + market` | 25,072 | 622 | 622 |
| `order_id + customer_id` | 25,753 | 0 | 1 |
| `order_id + customer_id + order_date` | 25,754 | 0 | 0 |

`order_id` alone is **not** unique to an order: 659 ids appear against two
different customers on two different dates. **Decision:** the order key is the
composite `(order_id, customer_id, order_date)`, giving **25,754** orders
rather than 25,035 - a 2.87% difference that flows
straight into average order value and order-frequency metrics.

## 5. Product identity

- Distinct `product_id`: **10,292**
- Distinct `product_name`: **3,788**
- Distinct `(product_id, product_name)` pairs: **10,768**
- `product_id`s carrying more than one name: **457**
- `product_name`s carrying more than one id: **1,944**
- `product_id`s spanning more than one category: **0**

Neither field is a clean key, but `product_id` is the better one: it never
crosses a category boundary (0 violations), whereas 1,944 names
are reused across genuinely different ids. **Decision:** aggregate on `product_id`,
and attach `product_name` = the most frequent name for that id so reports carry a
readable, stable label. The 457 ambiguous ids are flagged in `products.csv` via
`name_variants` so no reader mistakes the label for a guarantee.

## 6. Discount precision

- Distinct raw values: **29**
- After `round(2)`: **23**
- After `round(3)`: **27**
- Float artifacts present: `0.15000000000000002`, `0.44999999999999996`, `0.47000000000000003`, `0.5700000000000001`
- Genuine three-decimal levels: ['0.002', '0.202', '0.402', '0.602'], covering **629** order lines

**Decision: round to 3dp.** This removes the binary-representation artifacts
while preserving the 4 real three-decimal discount tiers. Rounding to
2dp - the reflexive choice - would collapse those into 0.00/0.20/0.40/0.60 and
silently destroy a real pricing distinction on 629 rows.

## 7. Dates and delivery

- `order_date` range: **2011-01-01** to **2014-12-31** (4 full calendar years)
- Unparseable dates: **0** (both columns arrive as real datetimes)
- `ship_date` earlier than `order_date`: **0**
- Order-to-ship lag: min **0**, mean **3.97**, max **7** days

**Caveat carried forward:** the workbook has a *ship* date, not a *delivery*
date. Everything downstream therefore measures **order-to-ship lag**, and the
spec's "average delivery time" is reported under that honest name. Actual
transit time is not in this dataset and is not estimated.

## 9. Deriving cost

The dataset has no `cost_price`, but it has real `profit`, so cost is not
assumed - it is derived exactly:

```
cost = revenue - profit
```

- Resulting cost range: 0.5544 to 24,449.5584
- Negative (impossible) costs: **0**
- Total cost: **11,175,044.62**

Because profit is genuine, every margin, profitability and cost figure in this
project is a real measurement rather than a modelled assumption.

## 10. Is `revenue` gross or net of discount?

Tested on 6,671 products sold both at full price and
at a discount, comparing observed unit price against each hypothesis:

| Hypothesis | Median absolute error |
|---|---:|
| `revenue` is NET of discount (unit = list x (1-d)) | 0.00% |
| `revenue` is GROSS of discount (unit = list) | 40.00% |

**Verdict: `revenue` is NET of discount.** This is tested rather than
assumed because the whole discount-effectiveness analysis depends on it:
`discount_amount` and gross/list revenue are reconstructed from this relationship.

## 11. Returns coverage - a real limit on the analysis

- `Returned` only ever takes the value ['Yes'] - there is no explicit 'No'
- Market labels differ between sheets: Returns says `United States`, Orders says `US` (normalised before joining)
- Markets **with** return records: APAC, EU, LATAM, US
- Markets with **zero** return records: **Africa, Canada, EMEA**

| Join key | Order lines flagged | Row count preserved |
|---|---:|---|
| `order_id` only (naive) | 3,050 | yes |
| `(order_id, market)` (used) | 3,043 | yes |

The naive join over-flags by 7 lines, because a reused `order_id` in an uncovered market inherits another market's return.

**Decision and limitation.** Return rate is computed **only** over APAC, EU, LATAM, US. For Africa, Canada, EMEA the return rate is recorded as
*unknown*, never as 0%. Reporting zero would invent a flawless returns record for
three markets that simply were not measured, and would understate the global rate.

## 12. Brand - a derived field, declared as derived

The spec asks for `brand`; the dataset has no such column. The first token of
`product_name` is a manufacturer with high reliability, so it is extracted:

- Distinct brands derived: **467**
- Brands on >=100 order lines: **78**, covering **91.8%** of lines
- Lines where the token is numeric/punctuation and set to `Unknown`: **111** (0.22%)
- Most frequent: Avery, Eldon, Hon, Tenex, Smead, Xerox, Acco, Wilson, Fellowes, Novimex, Harbour, Cardinal

**Stated limitations.** Multi-word brands truncate to their first word
(`Binney` for "Binney & Smith", `Harbour` for "Harbour Creations"). Roughly 3.7%
of `product_id`s disagree internally on the token. `brand` is therefore usable for
ranking and grouping, but it is a **derivation, not source data**, and is labelled
as such wherever it appears.

## 13. Spec requirements this dataset cannot support

These were **omitted and explained rather than fabricated**:

| Requested | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `gender`, `age` | **Omitted** | No demographic fields exist. Any values would be invented. |
| `payment_method` | **Omitted** | Not recorded. The spec's payment-method analysis is therefore not answerable. |
| `order_status`, cancellation rate | **Omitted** | No status column. `order_priority` is a priority, not a status; returns are the only post-sale outcome recorded. |
| `signup_date` | **Derived, censored** | Taken as each customer's first observed order. Anyone active before 2011-01-01 is left-censored, so tenure is a floor, not a fact. |
| `delivery_date` | **Substituted** | Only `ship_date` exists; reported as order-to-ship lag. |
| `brand` | **Derived** | Extracted from `product_name` (section 12). |
| Return rate for Africa / Canada / EMEA | **Unknown** | No return records for those markets (section 11). |
